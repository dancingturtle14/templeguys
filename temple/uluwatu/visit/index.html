Pura Luhur Uluwatu, Bali

Desa Pecatu, Kuta Selatan, Badung Regency, Bali

Quick facts

Founded
Official temple page and Indonesia Travel date early evidence to a candi kurung chronogram of 808 Saka (about 886 AD). Development is often linked to Mpu Kuturan in the 11th century. The official temple page also honours Danghyang Nirartha, who is believed to have attained moksha on these cliffs.
Visit length
The official “7 things” visitor guide says: “Allow approximately one to two hours to walk through the visitor areas… Add more time when visiting during sunset hours or attending the Kecak Dance.” That is visitor-guide wording, not a timed ticket slot.
UNESCO
Not a UNESCO World Heritage listing
Patron / era
Sad Kahyangan sea temple, dedicated to Shiva Rudra

Visit

The official temple page and Indonesia Travel’s Indonesian pilgrimage page say the temple is open to visitors daily from 07:00 to 19:00 WITA. Admission is paid at the official booth near the main gate; the temple ticket is separate from Kecak and from parking. Published fares can change — confirm at the booth.

Tickets

Buy on site / verify

Preferred published temple fares as of 2026-11-12: international IDR 60,000 / 40,000; domestic IDR 40,000 / 30,000 — can change; official pages also still show older or different tables. Kecak and parking are separate. No official parking fare. No Bali temple pass.

Hours

The official temple page and Indonesia Travel’s Indonesian pilgrimage page say the temple is open to visitors every day from 07:00 to 19:00 WITA (“every day from 07:00 AM to 07:00 PM”; “Pura Uluwatu buka setiap hari pukul 07.00-19.00 WITA”). These are visitor opening hours, not office or contact hours. An Indonesia Travel heritage list still prints Uluwatu “Operating Hours: 09.00 - 19.00” — that is a conflicting official tourism-list figure; prefer 07:00–19:00 from the temple’s own visitor page, and confirm on the day. Do not treat Kecak times as general visiting hours: the official Kecak page lists an open gate at 16:00 and a show from 18:00 to 19:00 every day (Indonesia Travel ID: 16.00 / 18.00–19.00 WITA). Official pages advise arriving about an hour early, or before 17:00, if you also want Kecak; tickets may sell out in busy periods. Access to some areas may change during ceremonies or operational conditions — follow signs and staff. Nyepi closures have been announced year by year (see Notes).

Tickets

Paid. Preferred official “updated ticket pricing,” published on the temple page, the 2026 fee article, and Indonesia Travel’s Indonesian pilgrimage page (accessed 2026-11-12): international adult IDR 60,000 / child IDR 40,000; domestic adult IDR 40,000 / child IDR 30,000. Official pages say prices and arrangements may change — pay at the official ticket booth near the main gate, and re-check the official temple page (https://uluwatutemple.id/uluwatu-temple). The temple ticket includes the use of a sarong and sash. It does not include parking or the Kecak performance. Parking is a separate payment; no official parking fare was published on the official pages cited here. Official pages currently disagree on some published fares, so do not treat one table as the only figure a booth will charge. The official homepage FAQ, an older “ultimate guide” article, and the Indonesia Travel heritage list still show international IDR 50,000 / 30,000 and domestic IDR 30,000 / 20,000. Some English Indonesia Travel pilgrimage pages list domestic IDR 50,000 / 30,000 and foreign IDR 100,000 / 50,000, and a Kecak “as of 2025” foreign fare of IDR 200,000. Kecak is a separate product. Preferred official Kecak page and Indonesia Travel Indonesian pilgrimage page: adult IDR 150,000; child ages 2–9 IDR 75,000, generally cash-only. Temple entrance and parking are not included. The English pilgrimage “as of 2025” foreign IDR 200,000 line is a conflicting official tourism figure. Temple child age is not officially defined (only the Kecak 2–9 band is) — confirm at the official counter. Labuan Sait Beach, if visited, is another separate ticket on the same managing site; it is not part of the temple fare. There is no official Bali temple pass. There is no official paid e-ticket URL — official text mentions the booth and that “some platforms may offer pre-booking,” without naming an official booking page.

Dress

Sarong and sash are required in the temple area and are provided at the entrance (included with the ticket). Official visitor guidance still asks for modest clothing underneath, ideally covering shoulders and knees. Indonesia Travel (Indonesian pilgrimage): pakaian sopan that covers the body and suits a place of worship.

Duration

The official “7 things” visitor guide says: “Allow approximately one to two hours to walk through the visitor areas… Add more time when visiting during sunset hours or attending the Kecak Dance.” That is visitor-guide wording, not a timed ticket slot.

Access

Desa Pecatu, Kuta Selatan, Badung, Bali (no street-number address is published on the official pages cited here). Visitors use designated public pathways and viewpoints; do not enter sacred or restricted zones reserved for worshippers and authorised personnel. The 2021 Dinas Pariwisata Provinsi Bali destination page: “only a true Hindu follower can enter the second smaller temple inside.” Customary menstruation rule: prefer the official temple site and Indonesia Travel’s Indonesian pilgrimage page (“not allowed in the temple area / area pura sesuai aturan adat setempat”). The English pilgrimage page says “central area” instead — keep both official wordings; follow staff on the day. Facilities listed on official pages include toilets, wantilan / gazebos, parking, wheelchairs, a prayer area, first aid / ambulance, warungs and souvenir shops, an ATM, and an EV charging point near parking.

Tips

  • Pay at the official ticket booth near the main gate. Re-check current fares on the official site; published figures can change, and official pages currently disagree.
  • Bring cash. Kecak tickets are generally cash-only; the 2026 fee article also recommends cash for entrance and parking.
  • Wear the provided sarong and sash; arrive already in modest clothing.
  • If you plan to watch Kecak, arrive before 17:00 (gate 16:00; show 18:00–19:00). Official tourism warns that holiday-season late-afternoon traffic from Kuta or Seminyak can be heavy — leave early.
  • Secure glasses, hats, phones, and jewellery; do not feed or chase the monkeys; tell staff if something is taken.
  • Stay off boundary walls; the cliffs are steep. Do not bring food or drinks into the temple area. No drones unless authorised.
