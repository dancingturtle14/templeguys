Pura Luhur Uluwatu, Bali

Desa Pecatu, Kuta Selatan, Badung Regency, Bali

Quick facts

Founded
Official temple page and Indonesia Travel date early evidence to a candi kurung chronogram of 808 Saka (about 886 AD). Development is often linked to Mpu Kuturan in the 11th century. The official temple page also honours Danghyang Nirartha, who is believed to have attained moksha on these cliffs.
Visit length
The official “7 things” visitor guide says: “Allow approximately one to two hours to walk through the visitor areas… Add more time when visiting during sunset hours or attending the Kecak Dance.” That is visitor-guide wording, not a timed ticket slot.
UNESCO
Not a UNESCO World Heritage listing
Patron / era
Sad Kahyangan sea temple, dedicated to Shiva Rudra

Visit

Arrive at the main gate, buy the temple ticket at the official booth, and put on the sarong and sash provided with it before you walk in. From there the visit follows the cliff paths at your own pace. The official temple page and Indonesia Travel’s Indonesian pilgrimage page give opening hours as daily 07:00–19:00 WITA. The temple ticket covers the precinct only: Kecak and parking are paid separately, and Labuan Sait Beach, if you go, is another ticket again.

Tickets

Buy on site / verify

Preferred published temple fares: international IDR 60,000 / 40,000; domestic IDR 40,000 / 30,000 — can change; official pages also still show older or different tables. Kecak and parking are separate. No official parking fare. No official e-ticket URL. No Bali temple pass.

Hours

The official temple page and Indonesia Travel’s Indonesian pilgrimage page both state that the temple is open to visitors every day from 07:00 to 19:00 WITA. These are visitor opening hours, not office or contact hours. An Indonesia Travel heritage list still prints “Operating Hours: 09.00 - 19.00”; that is a conflicting official tourism-list figure, so prefer 07:00–19:00 from the temple’s own visitor page and confirm on the day. Kecak runs on its own clock. The official Kecak page lists an open gate at 16:00 and a performance from 18:00 to 19:00 every day, and official guides recommend arriving at the temple before 17:00 if you want both the precinct and the show. Do not read 16:00 or 18:00 as general visiting hours. Access to some areas may change during ceremonies or for operational reasons; follow signs and staff. Nyepi closures are announced year by year, and the 2025 notice is the only one published on the official pages cited here (see Notes).

Tickets

Admission is paid. The preferred published fares, listed on the temple page, in a 2026 fee article, in the official combined guide dated on the sitemap 2026-11-19, and on Indonesia Travel’s Indonesian pilgrimage page, are: international adult IDR 60,000 and child IDR 40,000; domestic adult IDR 40,000 and child IDR 30,000. Official pages say prices and arrangements may change, so pay at the official ticket booth near the main gate and re-check https://uluwatutemple.id/uluwatu-temple before you travel. Official pages currently disagree, so no single table should be treated as the only figure a booth will charge. The official homepage FAQ, an older “ultimate guide” article, and the Indonesia Travel heritage list still show international IDR 50,000 / 30,000 and domestic IDR 30,000 / 20,000. Some English Indonesia Travel pilgrimage pages list domestic IDR 50,000 / 30,000 and foreign IDR 100,000 / 50,000. Where an English and an Indonesian page differ, the Indonesian pilgrimage page and the temple’s own fare table are the ones to prefer. The temple ticket includes the use of a sarong and sash. It does not include parking, and it does not include Kecak. Parking is paid separately; no official parking fare was published on the pages cited here. There is no official Bali temple pass and no official paid e-ticket URL — official text mentions the booth and notes only that some platforms may offer pre-booking, without naming an official booking page. SanctaWay does not sell tickets. Kecak is a separate product with its own price. Prefer the official Kecak page: adult IDR 150,000, child ages 2–9 IDR 75,000, with no WNA/WNI split, generally cash-only. An English Indonesia Travel pilgrimage page carries a Kecak “as of 2025” foreign fare of IDR 200,000; treat that as a conflicting official tourism figure and check the Kecak page. The temple child age band is not defined on the official pages; only Kecak defines child as ages 2–9. If you are buying for a child, confirm the band at the official counter. Labuan Sait Beach, also widely known as Padang-Padang Beach on the official managing site, is a further separate ticket on the same site and is not part of temple admission.

Dress

A sarong and sash are required in the temple area and are provided at the entrance with the ticket. Official visitor guidance asks for modest clothing underneath, ideally covering shoulders and knees. Indonesia Travel’s Indonesian pilgrimage page puts it as pakaian sopan — clothing that covers the body and suits a place of worship. The official “7 things” visitor guide also recommends comfortable walking shoes: the visitor route includes steps, stone pathways, uneven surfaces, and long cliffside walking sections.

Duration

The official “7 things” visitor guide says: “Allow approximately one to two hours to walk through the visitor areas… Add more time when visiting during sunset hours or attending the Kecak Dance.” That is visitor-guide advice, not a timed slot on a ticket.

Access

Desa Pecatu, Kecamatan Kuta Selatan, Kabupaten Badung, Bali. No street-number address is published on the official pages cited here. Visitors use the designated public pathways and viewpoints; sacred and restricted zones are for worshippers and authorised personnel. The 2021 Dinas Pariwisata Provinsi Bali destination page states that only a true Hindu follower may enter the second smaller temple inside. On the customary menstruation rule, the official temple site and Indonesia Travel’s Indonesian pilgrimage page say it is not permitted in the temple area / area pura sesuai aturan adat setempat. The English pilgrimage page instead says “central area.” Keep both official wordings in mind and follow staff on the day; staff, not a webpage, decide at the gate. Facilities listed on official pages include public toilets, bale and gazebos (shaded resting areas), a wantilan, parking, wheelchair access, a prayer room, first aid and ambulances, warungs and souvenir shops, an ATM, and an EV charging station. The Indonesian pilgrimage page also mentions transport services within certain parts of the area, without describing a public-bus corridor.

Tips

  • Buy at the official ticket booth near the main gate; re-check fares on the official site, since published figures change and official pages disagree.
  • Bring cash. Kecak is generally cash-only, and the 2026 fee article also recommends cash for entrance and parking.
  • Wear the provided sarong and sash, and arrive already in modest clothing covering shoulders and knees.
  • If you want Kecak, arrive before 17:00 — gate 16:00, show 18:00–19:00. Holiday-season late-afternoon traffic from Kuta or Seminyak can be heavy; official text says to leave early.
  • Secure glasses, hats, phones, and jewellery. Do not feed, touch, catch, chase, or tease the monkeys; if one takes something, do not chase it — tell staff. First aid is at the visitor centre.
  • Stay off boundary walls; the cliffs are steep. No food or drinks in the temple area. No drones unless authorised.
  • Do not sit or climb on walls for photographs, and give worshippers and any procession space.
